# ChronoFlow: An Electricity-Free, Volumetric, Clockwork-Governed Syringe Driver for Neonatal Infusion in Low-Resource Settings

**Category:** Biomedical Engineering (ENBM)

**Purpose.** Neonatal IV therapy in hospitals without reliable electricity relies on gravity drips or hand-pushed syringes, and the only non-electric alternatives meter flow by pressure through a restriction, so delivered rate changes with fluid viscosity, temperature and back-pressure. The goal was to design a non-electric syringe driver that meters *volume* to ±5 %, works in any orientation, offers selectable clinical rates (1–10 mL/h), runs a full syringe on one wind, and has mechanical occlusion and free-flow protection, for under $50.

**Procedure.** A parametric mechanical design was developed in CadQuery (66 parts, STEP/STL exported) and validated with physics-based models written for the project: torsion of thin pre-tensioned strips for the balance suspension, beam-column finite elements for an alternative flexure pivot, a fourth-order Runge–Kutta simulation of balance–escapement dynamics, Lewis tooth stress, a 20 000-sample Monte-Carlo error budget, and a pairwise solid-interference check of the assembly.

**Results.** Energy analysis showed the escapement must operate over an 18.4× power range (15–275 µW), which a conventional lever escapement cannot do without over-banking (simulated amplitude > 1.2 rad). A constant-force "spring-arm" escapement, adapted from the gravity escapement of tower clocks, holds amplitude constant at 0.59 rad and keeps period error below 0.06 % across the full range. A taut-band suspension made from a 0.04 mm feeler-gauge steel ribbon gives a 1.20 Hz balance with ±0.16 % orientation sensitivity, no pivot friction and infinite fatigue life (154 MPa vs 350 MPa limit). Plug-in gear cassettes provide 1, 2, 5, 10 mL/h (0.4–19.5 mL/h across 5–60 mL syringes) with 0.12–1.16 µL per-beat resolution. Predicted accuracy: 95 % of units within ±1.14 % of set rate, versus +45 % (37 °C) and −33 % (100 mmHg back-pressure) for flow-restriction pumps. Occlusion trips at 300 mmHg and stops the oscillator; a latch interlock prevents free-flow. Bill of materials: $38.

**Conclusion.** Combining two historical precision-mechanics principles — the constant-force escapement and the taut-band suspension — with 3D printing yields a printable, electricity-free infusion driver whose predicted accuracy is limited by the syringe bore rather than by fluid, temperature, pressure or orientation. The next step is the gravimetric validation protocol (IEC 60601-2-24) included with the design.
